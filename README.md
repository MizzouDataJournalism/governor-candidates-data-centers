# Where do your gubernatorial candidates stand on data center issues?
*By Maddie Carr, Statehouse Reporting Project*

The Statehouse Reporting Project sought to determine the views of candidates for governor on the issue of data centers by contacting each campaign and reviewing candidate campaign websites, press releases, debates, news articles, campaign ads, votes on legislation, governor websites and public comments. We cite the main source used in these evaluations, though many sources were reviewed.

The project looked at major candidates, defined as those polling 15 percent or more in polling data for the 36 gubernatorial races. Each candidate was evaluated on five data center topics: support for state-wide moratoriums, public water-use reporting, regulatory frameworks for power use, tax incentives and noise assessment requirements. 

Reporters made multiple attempts to reach candidates and their campaign offices via email for responses. Campaigns were notified of each stance the project recorded for their candidate. We asked candidates five questions associated with our evaluation. Does the candidate support: 
1. a state-wide moratorium?
2. public reporting requirements for water use? 
3. separate utility rates and use requirements for data centers?
4. noise assessment requirements for proposed data centers?
5. tax incentives for data centers?

Only three candidates responded to our requests. All three stated they would support separate utility rates and use requirements for data centers. The final database expands our definition to include support for any power regulations, including “bring your own energy” frameworks and full coverage of energy costs. 

A candidate's position was considered unclear on an issue if they did not respond to our requests and their stance was not addressed in public comments or voting records. Candidates who have not explicitly supported a moratorium (yes) or emphasized local control instead to counter a state-wide framework (no) were considered to have an unclear position on data center moratoriums. 

If candidates’ position on a topic becomes clearer or changes, the Statehouse Reporting Project plans to update the website database.
